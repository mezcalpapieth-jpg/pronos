What are prediction markets

A prediction market turns opinions into prices. On Pronos, those prices also become part of competition: you can buy positions, sell before close, build combo slips, and earn tournament score from conviction or liquidity.

PriceThe probability implied by the market.
LiquidityWhat lets users enter or exit without moving price too much.
OutcomeThe official source that decides who wins.

Cap 01

The core idea

Every market asks a verifiable question: if the event happens, one side wins; if it does not, the other side wins.

When you buy Yes, you are buying a position that pays if the event happens. When you buy No, you are buying a position that pays if the event does not happen. Before resolution, price moves with demand, supply, and available liquidity.

Yes62%The market thinks the event has a 62% chance.
No38%The opposite side completes 100%.

If you buy at 62 and the position wins, each share finishes at 100. If it loses, it finishes at 0. The difference between your entry price, your exit price, and the final outcome is your PnL.

Cap 02

Price, liquidity and fills

Price does not move randomly: it moves when someone consumes liquidity or leaves orders for others.

On Pronos, execution can come from two places: available order-book liquidity and automatic market depth. If there is a good resting order, your trade can take it. If that is not enough, the rest executes against the market curve.

What it means

The larger a trade is relative to available liquidity, the more it moves price. More liquidity means smoother movement and lower slippage.

Fill

The part of your order that executed. One trade can have multiple fills at different prices.

Slippage

The difference between the price you expected and the average execution price.

Top holders

Shows the users with the largest exposure marked to current price or, after resolution, the final result.

Key idea: if one side has more money and more positions, the price is not frozen. It means the other side needs more flow to move it in a reasonable way.
Cap 03

How wins and losses work

You can profit by being right at resolution or by selling after the market moves in your favor.

Buy a position

Choose a market, outcome, and amount. The system shows estimated price and expected movement.

The market moves

News, trades, and liquidity change probability. Your PnL can move before the market closes.

Sell or hold

You can exit early if there is liquidity, or hold until resolution and collect if you win.

It resolves

The defined source decides the outcome. The winning side pays; the losing side goes to zero.

For tournament markets, only eligible markets inside the cycle window count. Hidden, test, or non-tournament markets should not count toward your 10 qualifying markets or leaderboard PnL.

Cap 04

Resolution and sources

A good market is not only interesting: it has a clear rule for deciding the result.

Every market should say which source is used, when the data is read, and what happens in edge cases. Some markets resolve automatically with APIs or feeds like Chainlink; others need manual review because the source is unstructured or potentially ambiguous.

Automatic

Price, score, weather, or structured data. The system can resolve without intervention if evidence matches.

Review

News, nominations, text, or sources that require human confirmation.

Correction

If a resolution was wrong, it should be corrected with evidence and shown to users as a resolution change.

Before buying, always read the resolution rule. It matters more than the headline.
Cap 05

Combo slips

A combo slip combines several markets into one ticket. To win, every leg must be right.

Combos let you express a stronger thesis: for example, that three different events will all happen. Because that is harder to hit, the multiplier is higher. The risk is higher too: if one leg fails, the ticket loses.

3 to 6 markets

In the tournament, a combo uses 3 to 6 eligible markets. You cannot duplicate exposure from the same market group.

Multiplier

Payout uses fair odds times 0.75, with multiplier and payout caps to avoid extreme tickets.

Counts on settlement

An open combo does not move the leaderboard. PnL counts when the ticket is won, lost, or refunded.

Mental rule: use combos when your picks are tied to a clear thesis. If you are only chasing a large multiplier, you are mostly buying variance.
Cap 06

Conviction or long hold

The tournament rewards users who buy well and hold a winning position to resolution, not only users who trade all day.

The conviction multiplier applies to winning lots bought at 0.85 or below and held until resolution. It can add up to 1.5x on that lot's profit, with a cap: the bonus cannot exceed 50% of your net PnL in that market.

Buy with margin

Very expensive entries have less room for bonus. The system rewards good entry price, not chasing the final point.

Hold to resolution

If you sell before resolution, that lot no longer qualifies as a conviction lot.

Only if it wins

There is no bonus for holding a losing position. Conviction matters when you were also right.

Limit orders that fill can count for conviction if they become winning buy lots and are held through resolution.

Cap 07

Maker liquidity

You can also support a market by leaving limit orders near the current price.

A limit order can help other users enter or exit with less slippage. In the tournament, orders near the live price can earn liquidity rewards at a 20% weekly rate, prorated daily and capped per user per day.

Maker

You leave an order and wait for someone to take it. If it fills, you own a position at the price you offered.

Taker

You buy or sell using existing liquidity. It is more immediate, but it can move price more.

Providing liquidity does not remove risk. If your order fills, you have a real market position.
Cap 08

What else to know

These are the pieces worth understanding before competing seriously.

Eligibility

Rewards can be withheld or canceled for abuse, multiple accounts, unauthorized automation, or terms violations.

Qualifying markets

The ranking requires 10 eligible markets. The count uses tournament markets, not every historical market.

Inactivity

A small penalty can apply for inactive days during the cycle.

History

Past leaderboards remain visible below as historical top 10s.

Price risk

An 80% price is not a guarantee. It is the market's current consensus.
